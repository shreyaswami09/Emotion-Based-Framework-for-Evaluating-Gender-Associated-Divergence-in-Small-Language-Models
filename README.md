## Ethics of AI: A Framework for Evaluating Gender-Associated Emotional Divergence in Small Language Models
This paper presents a methodology to statistically evaluate the gender-associated emotional divergence from small language model responses through replication and multiple-testing correction, to compliment existing bias-evaluation approaches with a new perspective and focusing specifically on SLMs rather than LLMs, like the majority of frameworks.

## Objective
Recently, there has been a surge in usage of language models due to the plummeted token costs. But because of their cost-efficiency, localized and specialized nature, much of the industrial usage has shifted from large to small language models. However, the drastic increase in SLM interest has not been paralleled by equivalent unique evaluation in their multi-faceted ethical concerns. As a result, much of the current testing performed on SLM objectivity is carried out using LLM frameworks. This paper presents a statistical method of benchmarking to evaluate consistent divergence in SLM responses based on gender-association. Furthermore, this framework relies solely on emotional and sentiment connotations of responses, a relatively-less thoroughly researched aspect of existing SLM benchmarks. It consists of a procured prompt dataset consisting of paired prompts, differing only in gender-associated name. Through replication, t-testing, and multiple t-test correction, results reveal that the five SLMs selected for this study do not exhibit significant consistencies towards any gender based on emotional pull. Furthermore, the magnitudes of emotional differences between pairs were consolidated to produce emotion and sentiment metrics for the five SLMs. Since the metrics were not small enough to be negligible, their values indicate that even if there is no consistent skewing towards one gender, stochastic sporadicity may still contribute to a model's response leaning excessively far in different directions at different times. Although those might be outliers, they still hold potential risks for the integrity of companies employing them to one-time users who happen to receive a said outlier. Logical next steps of this experiment are to perform it with a much larger sample size, with multiple emotion evaluators, and should be replicated for LLMs to compare outputs and potentially reveal patterns that suggest SLMs should be evaluated differently.
